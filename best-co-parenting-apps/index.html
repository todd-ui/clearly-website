The 2026 co-parenting app comparison

The first generation of co-parenting apps made conflict visible. This generation makes it solvable.

Most co-parenting app comparisons are written by the apps themselves. So is this one.

We build Clearly. We are going to tell you when it is the right choice, and when it is not. The category changed this year, and most of what has been written about it is already wrong.

Built to resolve conversations, not host them. The first co-parenting communication system designed from the ground up to finish what other apps just store.

Comparison · Why it's different · FAQ · Pricing

The short answer.

If you're choosing freely, the right app depends on what's actually breaking. If communication is the problem, pick the app that changes how you talk to each other. If documentation for active litigation is the problem, pick the app courts already know. If scheduling is the only problem, you may not need a co-parenting app at all. Recommendations by situation are below.

What changed in 2026.

The first generation of these apps was built for a world that needed proof. They made conflict visible and time-stamped. That work mattered. It still matters in active litigation. But most co-parents are not in active litigation anymore. They are in a relationship that ended, with a relationship that didn't. A new generation of apps is being built for what comes after the documents are signed.

The free-tier era ended. AppClose, the default free option for nearly a decade, moved to a paid subscription on January 1, 2026. TalkingParents retired its free plan on March 30, 2026. Every comparison article written before that date is now wrong about pricing and recommendations.

Newer apps such as Clearly, BestInterest, Kidtime and PeacePath are building for what comes next. The incumbents have mostly added features to the same model and raised prices.

For twenty years, co-parenting apps were built to document the conflict.

Clearly is built to shift the patterns underneath it.

Records that hold up.

Clearly is built to resolve conversations, but when you need the record, it’s ready for court. Export the whole record or just the part that matters, from iPhone or any browser.

  • Checkable by anyone.

    Every page carries the document’s ID and its own page number. A judge, lawyer or the other parent can scan the code or drop the PDF into getclearly.app/verify, and Clearly confirms it made that exact file and nothing has changed.

  • Every word, as written.

    Messages can’t be edited or deleted by anyone, Clearly included. Each entry is numbered, timed by Clearly’s server and shows when the other parent first read it.

  • The whole record, or one exhibit.

    Everything at once, or narrowed to the dates, parent, topic or words that matter. The record includes a summary of parenting time from the schedule, and who owes whom.

  • Facts, never conclusions.

    The parents’ own words, with no scores or analysis. Children can appear as initials, and there’s a declaration to sign if your court asks for one.

Clearly

If communication is what's broken.

Clearly is a co-parenting app built on the Harvard Negotiation Project's framework for resolving conflict. When a conversation needs it, Clearly steps in like a mediator would: it helps word a hard message so it moves toward an answer. You choose what to send, and posts stay on the record as written. When you agree, Clearly writes up what you both agreed so you can each accept the same words.

The fights themselves don't get logged. They get interrupted. Most co-parenting apps assume the patterns you couldn't fix in the marriage will keep going on a new platform. Clearly assumes the opposite. Stonewalling, the silent treatment, the slow drip of hostility one message at a time: those don't work in a system designed to close the loop, not extend it. The calendar, expenses, and custody schedule work the way a well-designed app should. The logistics are the easy part. Shifting the patterns is the hard part. Clearly does both.

OurFamilyWizard

If a judge has told you which app to use.

Founded in 2001 and the most established app in the category, named in custody orders across the US. Family-law attorneys are most familiar with it because it's been around longest.

If your case is headed to court and your attorney has a preferred app, it's probably this one. Judges in many jurisdictions recognize it on sight. $110 to $300 per parent per year depending on tier, $220 to $600 for both.

TalkingParents

If you need an unalterable record of everything.

A communication-first platform that creates timestamped, unalterable records of every message, call, and exchange. Messages cannot be edited or deleted once sent.

The Accountable Calling feature records phone and video calls with transcripts that become part of the legal record. If a co-parent denies conversations happened or routinely edits their version of events, TalkingParents removes that argument. $77 to $352 per parent per year depending on tier, $154 to $704 for both.

AppClose

If you want all-in-one at the lowest paid price.

Messaging, calendar, expense tracking, and a closed-loop payment system that lives entirely inside the app. For a decade it was the free option in the category. Since January 1, 2026, it costs $7.99 a month per parent on the web ($8.99 in the app), or $83.88 a year prepaid.

Covers the most ground in a single app at a lower price than OurFamilyWizard. The expense and payment integration is functional. About $84 per parent per year, $168 for both.

Kidtime, BestInterest, PeacePath, 2houses, Custody X Change

The rest of the field.

Kidtime has a free tier with pre-built custody-schedule templates (Premium is listed at $99.99 per parent per year, $200 for both, often discounted). BestInterest added a free plan that never expires in January 2026, with paid plans from $99.99 a year per parent ($200 for both). PeacePath, a web app, positions on high-conflict situations. 2houses is the strongest option for international or bilingual co-parents, at $169.99 a year for the whole family. Custody X Change is built for parenting plans, not daily communication. None are built on a documented negotiation framework the way Clearly is.

Which app for which situation.

  1. Your custody order names a specific app.

    Use that app. This isn't a choice. Switching requires a motion and your co-parent's agreement or the court's.

  2. You're in active litigation or expect to be soon.

    OurFamilyWizard or TalkingParents are the safe defaults. Lawyers and judges have seen them most often. Clearly works here too: every record and exhibit carries a document ID and page number on every page, and anyone can check a copy at getclearly.app/verify. If your attorney is open to newer tools, it's worth the conversation.

  3. Your conversations keep going sideways.

    Clearly. Not every co-parent is in active hostility, but a lot of co-parents are exhausted by conversations that never reach a conclusion. Clearly is built to close the loop, so the back-and-forth doesn't drag.

  4. You need court-grade records of phone and video calls.

    TalkingParents. It's the only app with Accountable Calling.

  5. Expense tracking is the only thing you actually need.

    If expenses are genuinely the only thing, a tool like Splitwise will do the job. But most co-parenting expenses come with a conversation attached. Clearly logs the expense, tracks the split, and lets the other parent tap Pay to open Venmo, Cash App or PayPal with the amount filled in, then mark it paid. AppClose covers the same ground inside a closed-loop payment system.

  6. You and your co-parent are amicable and just need a shared calendar.

    You may not need a co-parenting app right now. Cozi, Google Calendar, or TimeTree will do the job. If you do want one place that holds the schedule plus expenses plus messages, Clearly's private calendar link adds your schedule to the calendar app you're already using, so you don't have to choose between them.

  7. Budget is the deciding factor.

    Kidtime and BestInterest both have free plans that don't expire. Both have limits, but they let you and your co-parent start without paying.

  8. You're on Android.

    Clearly has no Android app, but it works fully in your phone's browser at web.getclearly.app, with the same account and record as the iPhone app. If you want an app to download, OurFamilyWizard, TalkingParents, AppClose, BestInterest and Kidtime all have Android apps.

Frequently asked questions.

What is the best co-parenting app in 2026?

There isn't one best app, because the apps solve different problems. If your court order names an app, use that one. If active litigation is your context, OurFamilyWizard. If you need unalterable call records, TalkingParents. If the conversations themselves are what's broken, Clearly. If budget is the constraint, Kidtime or BestInterest still offer free tiers.

Is there still a free co-parenting app?

AppClose ended its free tier on January 1, 2026, and TalkingParents ended its free plan on March 30, 2026. As of October 2026, Kidtime and BestInterest both have free plans that don't expire. Generic family calendars like Cozi remain free but lack co-parenting-specific features.

Which co-parenting app do courts prefer?

Courts do not formally approve apps. In practice, OurFamilyWizard is named in custody orders more often than any other app, followed by TalkingParents and AppClose. This reflects familiarity, not endorsement. Newer apps are accepted in most jurisdictions when records are properly exported. Clearly's records carry a document ID and page number on every page, and anyone can check a copy is genuine at getclearly.app/verify.

What's the difference between OurFamilyWizard and Clearly?

OurFamilyWizard documents your messages and flags charged language with ToneMeter, asking you to rewrite them yourself. Clearly steps in only when a conversation needs it, helping word a hard message so it moves toward an answer, then writes up what you both agreed so you can each accept it. Every post stays on the record as written. OurFamilyWizard is built to document conflict. Clearly is built to resolve it.

What are the best OurFamilyWizard alternatives?

It depends on why you're leaving. If you need unalterable records of phone and video calls, TalkingParents is the only app with Accountable Calling ($77 to $352 per parent per year, $154 to $704 for both). If you want messaging, a calendar, expenses and in-app payments for less, AppClose covers the most ground at about $84 per parent per year ($168 for both). If budget is the constraint, Kidtime and BestInterest still offer free tiers. If the conversations themselves keep going sideways, Clearly steps in like a mediator when a conversation needs it and helps you both reach an answer, at $249 a year with one subscription covering both parents. If your custody order names OurFamilyWizard, keep using it.

TalkingParents vs OurFamilyWizard: which is better?

They solve different problems. OurFamilyWizard is the app lawyers and judges have seen most, and it is named in custody orders across the US; it costs $110 to $300 per parent per year, $220 to $600 for both parents. TalkingParents is the only app with Accountable Calling, which records phone and video calls with transcripts that become part of the legal record; it costs $77 to $352 per parent per year, $154 to $704 for both parents. If your case is in active litigation, OurFamilyWizard is the safe default. If your co-parent disputes what was said on calls, choose TalkingParents. Both are built to document conflict; if you want help resolving it, see how Clearly compares above.

Can I use a co-parenting app if my ex refuses to join?

Some apps offer one-sided modes. AppClose has Solo, which lets you send requests to a non-connected co-parent via text or email. Most apps require both parents to be subscribed for the full feature set. If your co-parent refuses and your custody order doesn't require an app, you may need to file a motion to have one ordered.

How much do co-parenting apps cost?

As of October 2026, the cost for both parents per year: AppClose $168, TalkingParents $154 to $704, OurFamilyWizard $220 to $600, BestInterest free or $200 to $600, Kidtime free or $200, 2houses $170. Clearly is $249, one subscription for both parents. OurFamilyWizard, TalkingParents and AppClose charge each parent separately.

How do yes-or-no questions work in Clearly?

Questions get a one-tap Yes or No. Instead of typing back a reply, your co-parent taps Yes or No, the answer is kept on the record, and the conversation keeps moving.

How does Clearly handle expense payments?

Clearly does not build its own payment system. Add your payment details once in Clearly. When your co-parent owes you for something, they tap Pay to open Venmo, Cash App or PayPal with the amount filled in, then mark it paid. No new bank link, no third payment processor.

Does Clearly's calendar work with my iPhone calendar?

Yes. Add your schedule to Apple or Google Calendar with a private calendar link, so your custody schedule lives alongside the rest of your life in the calendar app you already use. No switching back and forth between apps to see your week.
